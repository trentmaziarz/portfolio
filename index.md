<div class="about-text">
<p class="drop-cap">My name is Trent Maziarz. Computer Scientist, Buhurt Fighter, and the latest in a line of Polish tar-makers; my surname is "Maziarz" which means 'dealer in tar, wheel grease, or cart grease'. The irony is we went away from Poland to escape that trade work, we arrived in America where nobody needed wagon grease anymore, and the trade went to sleep in the name. Turns out I need it.</p>

<p><a class="marginalia-link" href="https://tenor.com/view/smirk-cat-smile-laugh-tricky-gif-14599063277834390205"><img class="marginalia marginalia-right marginalia-tilt-l" src="/assets/img/marginalia/cat.webp" alt="A smirking cat, drawn as manuscript marginalia"></a>Most days I write code. At 21 I founded a non-profit called Trace Foundation, where we made our first product <a href="https://www.aitrace.org/">AI Trace</a>, a public record of how companies use AI, and in my free time I point computer science at history, some would say, a digital history. I like to learn from the past, and often the future can be applied to it. I was inspired by a Software Engineering class when I was at UMASS Amherst, where a professor mentioned there was a talk happening on campus, a researcher was coming to talk about how they finally were able to "unravel" these old Pompeii scrolls using X-rays and AI, for an entirety of history it was impossible, they were folded amongst each other thinly (as was custom of the time), turned to ash and hardened to stone. Modern Computer Science made it possible to see another's life, and since then I use my hyperfixation to see what I can do myself. Temporal network analyses of the Black Plague on trade routes? NLP on old manuscripts?</p>

<p><img class="marginalia marginalia-wide marginalia-left" src="/assets/img/marginalia/knights.webp" alt="doodle of two knights fighting while a third lies flat on the ground, labeled me">Portuguese and Polish ancestry would never have expected that one day their kin would wear a suit of armor (we were quite poor historically on both sides), instead of shoveling stables like peasants or greasing wagons, I participate in Buhurt, meaning I dropped used car money to fight other grown adults in 60 pounds of armor until enough axe heads to the throat makes the other give out, or a leg gets broken. You'd think the fact when I'm 50 I probably will have knees made of glass would have steered me away, but lowkey an ice-cold beer after makes it worth it for now, and the people I've met and friends I've made keep me standing.</p>

<p><img class="marginalia marginalia-right marginalia-tilt-r" src="/assets/img/marginalia/jar.webp" alt="A jar of tar grease, drawn as manuscript marginalia">Though to bring it back to the tar, as I steered right past that, in my workshop I either produce grease for my armor / blades, and a version for leather if you so see fit, based on a old family recipe. I also make weapons and axes, but they didn't call me the sword-maker in my bloodline so they aren't as good as the grease.</p>
